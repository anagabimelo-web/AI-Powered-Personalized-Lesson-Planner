# Example in Practice

This is an anonymized example based on my experience as a private English teacher. No personally identifiable student information is included.

## 1. The Situation

I was working with a beginner English student who was having difficulty with traditional written activities. The student could participate more effectively when the activities were connected to personal interests and when the tasks were broken into smaller steps.

The learning goal was still important: the student needed to practice vocabulary, sentence formation, and basic reading skills.

The challenge was finding a way to work on these skills without making the lesson feel repetitive or discouraging.

## 2. How I Used AI

I provided the AI with relevant context about the student's level, learning goals, interests, and main challenges.

I asked it to generate several possible activities that could address the same learning objectives while making the lesson more engaging.

The AI suggested activities involving vocabulary, sentence building, and a short reading task connected to the student's interests.

## 3. What the AI Suggested

One of the suggested activities was to introduce new vocabulary and then ask the student to use the words to create simple sentences.

For example:

**player + ball**

→ "The player has a ball."

The AI also suggested gradually introducing a short reading activity using the same vocabulary.

## 4. My Adaptation

I did not use the AI-generated plan exactly as provided.

Based on my experience with the student, I adapted the activity by introducing the vocabulary visually first and turning the sentence-building exercise into a more interactive challenge.

I also reduced the amount of new vocabulary and adjusted the difficulty of the sentences to match the student's current ability.

This allowed me to keep the original learning objective while making the activity more appropriate for the student.

## 5. What I Learned

This experience showed me that AI is most useful when I provide meaningful context and use its output as a starting point rather than a final answer.

The AI helped me generate possibilities quickly, but the teacher's understanding of the student was essential for deciding which ideas were actually appropriate.

The experience also made me think more broadly about how the same workflow could be applied to other situations:

**Collect context → identify needs → generate possibilities → review → personalize → observe results → improve.**

This is the approach I want to continue exploring as I learn more about AI and how it can support people and improve everyday workflows.
